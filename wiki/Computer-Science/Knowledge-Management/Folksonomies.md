Folksonomies are user-generated classification systems where individuals apply free-form keywords, or tags, to digital content instead of using a rigid, top-down structure

**Loose Ontologies**

see https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folksonomy

## Key Characteristics

- **User-Driven:** Content is tagged by everyday users and consumers rather than formal subject experts or content creators.
- **Non-Hierarchical:** Unlike formal taxonomies, there are no rigid parent-child categories or strict organizational trees.
- **Evolving:** The classification system grows and adapts organically as more people interact with and label the content.

## Common Examples

- **Social Bookmarking & Media:** Platforms like [Del.icio.us](../Software/Social%20Software/Del.icio.us.md) and  [Flickr](https://www.flickr.com/) or image repositories where users attach descriptive keywords to photos. 
	- see [Social Bookmarking](Social%20Bookmarking.md)
- **Hashtags:** Content tags used across social media platforms like X (formerly Twitter) to categorize trending discussions. 
- **Collaborative Platforms:** Community-driven libraries or fan-fiction archives (like Archive of Our Own) that rely on community-voted tags. 

## Benefits and Drawbacks

- **Flexibility:** Adapts instantly to new slang, trends, and vocabulary without waiting for structural updates.
- **Ambiguity:** Can suffer from spelling variations, synonyms (e.g., "pics" vs. "photos"), and subjective interpretations that make strict retrieval difficult.
